BASELINE READING · AUGUST 2026

What the baseline says

This is a decision baseline, not a forecast engine. The reading should move only when the evidence changes.

01

Entry is under strain

Global youth outcomes, graduate transitions and junior hiring remain weaker than a healthy first-rung system would suggest.

02

Work organization changed the first rung

Remote and hybrid work predate generative AI and can reduce proximity, feedback and the ease with which inexperienced workers are developed.

03

An AI contribution remains plausible

Age × exposure and substitutability patterns remain suggestive, but working-from-home exposure materially complicates simple AI attribution.

04

Capability formation is the strategic issue

The risk is not only fewer jobs. It is fewer opportunities to enter, learn through work and become the experienced workers firms later expect to hire.

OCTOBER 2026 · RESEARCH UPDATE

What has changed since August?

New administrative, payroll, recruitment and firm-level evidence brings the first rung into sharper focus. These are findings from different studies and populations, not one continuous series or a causal verdict on AI.

ONS estimates for July–September put UK vacancies 5.1% below the previous year. This is a wider-market context measure, not a first-rung or AI-specific indicator.

EARLY-CAREER HIRING · US−12%

Recruitment may be the first adjustment

Employment for ages 22–24 in the most AI-exposed industry-state groups fell relative to less-exposed groups over ten quarters. The decline was driven largely by weaker hiring.

Association; pre-trends and remote work matter. Tucker (2026) · Source ↗
GRADUATE DESTINATIONS · US−5 pp / −13%

A job is not necessarily a first rung

For the most exposed college majors, initial employment probability and earnings fell relative to other majors. Part of the earnings loss reflected movement into lower-paying sectors.

Computing-heavy exposure; technology-sector correction is a rival explanation. Orr, Tucker & Warren (2026) · Source ↗
INCUMBENT EMPLOYERS · TEXAS−8–9%

Existing firms are changing what they recruit for

Online job postings fell relatively more at Texas firms whose previous vacancies were more exposed to AI automation, even among businesses present throughout the period.

Job postings measure advertised demand, not hires or AI-caused displacement. Dodini & Smith (2026) · Source ↗
COURSE CHOICES · TEXAS−4.8%

The signal may be reaching university admissions

Undergraduate enrollment shifted away from more AI-exposed majors in 2024–25. Graduates from those majors also experienced weaker Texas employment and earnings outcomes.

Relative to a 10-point exposure difference; student motivation was not directly measured. Dodini & Smith (2026) · Source ↗
FIRM ADOPTION · US1–6%

Exposure explains surprisingly little about adoption

Across fourteen measures, occupational AI exposure explained a small fraction of differences in reported firm adoption. Organizational response matters, although the available measures remain imperfect.

Binary adoption, single-unit firms; not an estimate of AI's employment effects. Dey et al. (2026) · Source ↗
TASK INVENTORIES · US79 new

Exposure measures depend on what the inventory misses

Worker-grounded TaskNet inventories identify 79 additional tasks across six occupations. More complete descriptions change estimates of what AI could do, before considering whether firms actually reorganize work.

Methodological evidence only; no observation of junior task allocation or employment effects. Wang et al. (2026) · Source ↗
EU COUNTER-SIGNAL · JRCρ −0.14

Exposure is a weak guide to observed labor demand

Across 101 European occupations, the relationship between AI exposure and 2019–24 job-advertisement growth is weak and statistically insignificant. The most-exposed occupational quintile also grew in employment during 2023–24.

Earlier period, experimental online-vacancy data; neither result tests recent junior hiring directly. JRC, AI and Skills (2026) · Source ↗
COUNTER-SIGNAL AND ATTRIBUTION

Do not mistake exposure for displacement

Bonney and colleagues found that only 2% of US firms reported employment reductions associated with AI in their 2025–26 survey, although broader operational deployment was associated with reductions in regression models. Exposure, use, integration, hiring and job quality remain distinct. The two Dallas Fed articles also use related data and should not be counted as two independent confirmations. The action case may strengthen without settling AI causality.

Bonney et al. (2026) · Source ↗

ATTRIBUTION CHECK

Remote work changes the causal story

Remote and hybrid work predate generative AI and affect many of the same occupations. The evidence now makes work organization both a developmental mechanism and a first-order competing explanation for weaker early-career hiring.

CURRENT READING

Remote work weakens simple AI attribution but strengthens the first-rung argument. If reduced proximity makes junior workers harder to develop, and AI then removes or redesigns some of the tasks through which they learn, organizations can rationally shift from building capability toward buying experience.

SIGNATURE MONITOR

The action threshold

An action threshold is reached when deterioration appears across multiple distinct evidence streams, a plausible mechanism links part of it to the suspected driver, competing explanations do not account for the full pattern, and the cost of delay may be difficult to reverse.

01 · Deterioration

Multiple streams

Payrolls, vacancies, official outcomes, employer reports and entrant surveys show related forms of strain.

READING · MET
02 · Mechanism

Two plausible pathways

Reduced coworker proximity can weaken feedback and development; AI can reduce or redesign junior tasks. Both can thin learning-by-doing without a wave of layoffs.

READING · MET
03 · Alternatives

Material competing explanations

Weak growth and work reorganization explain important parts of the pattern. AI exposure is not a clean treatment when it overlaps with working-from-home exposure.

READING · MATERIAL
04 · Delay cost

Capability can be lost

If firms thin the junior layer without replacing its developmental function, future experience pools weaken.

READING · MET

Current editorial judgment: the threshold for action has been crossed; the threshold for assigning primary causality to AI has not.

FIRST-RUNG MONITOR

The capability ladder

Entry-level work produces output, but it also produces experienced workers. The central question is whether new role designs preserve or replace that developmental function.

WORKING INTERPRETATION

The strategic question is not office versus remote or human versus AI. It is whether work design still produces experienced workers. Obsolete tasks and unnecessary presence can go; their developmental function still has to be replaced.

OPERATING MODEL

How the Observatory works

The website is the visible layer. The research asset is the evidence ledger, source register and documented judgment process beneath it.

01

Scan regularly

Check standing sources for genuinely new evidence on entry access, work organization, AI exposure, employer behavior and early-career capability.

02

Queue, don't auto-publish

Review new items for population, comparator, materiality, methodological quality, overlapping treatments and reuse of existing datasets.

03

Classify evidence

Tier 1 official/administrative; Tier 2 large platform or sector data; Tier 3 rigorous research or synthesis.

04

Keep provenance

Every observation retains source, date, comparator, caveat, AI specificity and the reason it matters. Exposure studies are checked explicitly for confounding and co-treatment.

05

Track counter-signals

Recovery evidence is not noise. It is required to test whether the entry-level problem persists as the wider market changes.

06

Separate action from causality

The site can justify monitoring and work redesign before it can justify assigning a definitive causal share to AI, remote work or any other single driver.

Important: “Action threshold crossed” is an editorial judgment about risk management under uncertainty. It is not a causal estimate and it does not imply that every country, sector or young worker is experiencing the same conditions.
