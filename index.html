When is the evidence enough?

The Observatory tracks whether labour-market entry is weakening, where AI is plausibly contributing, and whether the evidence has crossed a threshold for action without pretending that causality is settled.

CURRENT READING

What the baseline says

This is a decision baseline, not a forecast engine. The reading should move only when the evidence changes.

01

Entry is under strain

Global youth outcomes, graduate transitions and UK/US junior hiring are weaker than a healthy first-rung system would suggest.

02

The AI signal is real but bounded

Age × exposure and exposure × substitutability patterns are difficult to dismiss, but broad economic weakness still explains much of the aggregate slowdown.

03

Redesign is wider than replacement

Employer evidence points to rapid task and role change. Some substitution has begun, but wholesale entry-level collapse is not the modal finding.

04

The first rung is the strategic issue

The risk is not only fewer jobs. It is fewer opportunities to enter, learn through work and become the experienced workers firms later expect to hire.

SIGNATURE MONITOR

The action threshold

An action threshold is reached when deterioration appears across multiple distinct evidence streams, a plausible mechanism links part of it to the suspected driver, competing explanations do not account for the full pattern, and the cost of delay may be difficult to reverse.

01 · Deterioration

Multiple streams

Payrolls, vacancies, official outcomes, employer reports and entrant surveys show related forms of strain.

READING · MET
02 · Mechanism

Plausible AI contribution

Substitution of junior tasks can reduce hiring and weaken learning-by-doing without generating a wave of layoffs.

READING · MET
03 · Alternatives

Not fully explained

Cyclical weakness matters, but does not readily explain every age, exposure and substitutability pattern.

READING · PARTIAL
04 · Delay cost

Capability can be lost

If firms thin the junior layer without replacing its developmental function, future experience pools weaken.

READING · MET

Current editorial judgment: the threshold for action has been crossed; the threshold for assigning primary causality to AI has not.

OPERATING MODEL

How the Observatory works

The website is the visible layer. The research asset is the evidence ledger, source register and documented judgment process beneath it.

01

Scan regularly

Check standing sources for genuinely new evidence on entry access, AI exposure, employer behavior and early-career capability.

02

Queue, don't auto-publish

Review new items for population, comparator, materiality, methodological quality and overlap with existing evidence.

03

Classify evidence

Tier 1 official/administrative; Tier 2 large platform or sector data; Tier 3 rigorous research or synthesis.

04

Keep provenance

Every observation retains source, date, comparator, caveat, AI specificity and the reason it matters.

05

Track counter-signals

Recovery evidence is not noise. It is required to test whether the entry-level problem persists as the wider market changes.

06

Separate action from causality

The site can justify monitoring and redesign before it can justify assigning a definitive causal share to AI.

Important: “Action threshold crossed” is an editorial judgment about risk management under uncertainty. It is not a causal estimate and it does not imply that every country, sector or young worker is experiencing the same conditions.
